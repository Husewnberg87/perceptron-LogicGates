Why XOR fails on one perceptron

  • A single perceptron draws one straight line (w1·x1 + w2·x2 + b = 0) and says 1 on one side, 0 on the other.
  • AND and OR can be separated by a line, so the rule is guaranteed to converge (perceptron convergence theorem).
  • XOR needs the points (0,1) and (1,0) on one side and (0,0) and (1,1) on the other: no single line does that, so the weights keep cycling and errors never reach 0.
  • Fix: add a hidden layer. Here h1 = OR, h2 = NAND, and y = AND(h1, h2) is XOR. In the (h1, h2) plane the four points become separable by a line.
